## The six notes

### 1. `/lessons` index is fully redundant

> "The lessons page is fully redundant. All this information is shown on the main page."

**Cause:** `web/src/pages/Lessons.tsx` is the same three cards as the Lessons block in
`web/src/pages/Home.tsx`, with a different lede. The header nav links to it.

**Decision:** Delete the index route. `/lessons` redirects to `/` (deep links keep working);
the nav's "Lessons" entry goes away in favour of the map (note 4). Lesson pages keep their
`/lessons/*` routes.

### 2. Duelist anti-air doesn't read as a counter

> "Functionally this enemy is good. Visually he's just weird and it does not come across
> that he is counter-attacking when you jump in. It just looks like he is doing a
> stationary attack."

**Cause:** `drawDuelist` (`web/src/engine/render.ts`) draws the rising swipe as the same
crouch/arm pose as the lunge with `rise = -26`; the body never leaves the ground, the arms
barely move, and nothing points at the jumper. In the lesson demo
(`duelistAntiAirDemo`, `web/src/engine/demo.ts`) the ghost's arc lands *next to* the
duelist and nothing happens, so there is no cause→effect to read.

**Decision (visual + demo only — the state machine stays):** the anti-air gets its own
silhouette: a visible upward leap (body rises during `active`, returns in `recovery`), arms
thrown overhead as a clear upward arc, and the eyes/body track the jumper during the
telegraph. The demo's ghost arc is re-timed so the swipe visibly clips where the ghost
*was*, then the ghost lands behind and the gold window opens.

### 3. "Your Setup" should be lesson one, and needs pictures

> "1. This should be the first section because it will set up how she practices throughout
> the rest of the course. 2. This is another place that could use some visuals. Just showing
> the layout of the two controllers. We discuss the strengths and weaknesses of each and
> make sure to drive it home. It doesn't really matter which one she chooses as long as she
> just chooses one."

**Decision:** Setup becomes chapter 1 everywhere (map order, prev/next links, copy that
refers to "the other two lessons"). The page gains two inline SVG controller diagrams
(Joy-Con pair and a leverless/all-button layout) with labelled jump/attack/dash buttons, a
strengths/weaknesses pair, and a single closing line that drives the point home.

### 4. The landing page is boring — visuals over words, a map, "mini-games"

> "This landing page feels very boring to me. I think this can stand for here and the rest
> of the site. Let's do more visuals and fewer words. What if we have a map of Hollow Knight
> and she's going through different locations? The different chapters are the different
> lessons. Instead of calling things 'practice,' why don't we call them 'mini-games'? Make
> them fun."

**Decision (this is the M6 art direction, now with a brief):**
- Home becomes a **map of Hallownest-style journey**: hand-drawn-looking SVG map with
  locations as chapters, in teaching order. Each location is one lesson or mini-game; the
  path between them is the course. Fewer words everywhere — each location gets a name and
  one line, not a paragraph.
- **"Practice" → "Mini-games"** in nav, page titles, copy, and the HUD. Routes move to
  `/play/*`, with redirects from the old `/practice/*` paths so nothing already bookmarked
  breaks.
- "Fewer words" applies to the lesson pages too: trim ledes, lean on the demos and
  diagrams, keep the warm voice but at half the length.

### 5. Warden: shield placement and a missing threat

> "1. The warden should hold the shield above his head whenever he blocks from above to
> make it more clear that there is a weak spot in front of him. 2. If you just stand in
> front of him and jump or do nothing, then he actually never attacks, which I think is a
> big flaw. There's no threat unless you make a threat, which is not how the game works."

**Cause:** `resolveNailHit` (`web/src/engine/enemies.ts`) blocks *every* hit outside
recovery regardless of direction, and `stepWarden` has no proactive attack — its only
transition out of `idle` is the riposte triggered by a blocked hit.

**Decision (gameplay change, user-directed — PLAN §5 roster row updated):**
- **Positional shield.** The shield has an orientation, `front` or `up`, that tracks where
  the player is (above → `up`, else `front`) with a short re-aim delay. A hit from the
  *covered* side is blocked and provokes the riposte as before; a hit from the *uncovered*
  side lands. Real-game analog: the Colosseum's Shielded Fool, which blocks frontal hits
  and is open from above. The lesson: *watch where the shield is, hit where it isn't.*
- **Proactive shield bash.** If the player lingers in front within range for ~1.2 s, the
  warden telegraphs and delivers a short forward bash (new `AttackKind` `'bash'`), with its
  own recovery (gold, vulnerable). Standing still is no longer safe.
- Post-riposte/post-bash recovery stays vulnerable from every direction.

### 6. Jump height has a cliff between "short hop" and "full jump"

> "In the actual game, I don't think there are different jump heights that are
> predetermined, but there is a minimum jump height and a maximum jump height. You can get
> any value in between by changing how long you hold the button. … You can do some research
> and fact-check me if you want. Regardless, the distance between the highest short hop and
> the full hop feels like too much."

**Cause (confirmed in code):** `stepPlayer` (`web/src/engine/player.ts`) honours a jump
release only while the 0.18 s velocity pin is running. Release between 0.08–0.18 s gives a
continuous 53→120 px range; but once the pin expires the Knight goes ballistic at 666 px/s
and release is *ignored*, so the apex jumps from 120 px straight to 236 px. That 116 px gap
is the cliff the user felt.

**What the real game does:** `HeroController.JumpReleased()` zeroes `rb2d.velocity.y`
whenever the jump button is not held, vy > 0, and at least `JUMP_STEPS_MIN` steps have
elapsed — it runs every frame from `LookForInput()`, *not* only during the pinned phase. So
the user is right: the height is continuous from the minimum (4 pinned steps) to the
maximum (full ballistic ascent), and releasing at any point during the rise cuts it there.
(Fact-checked against the decompiled source this session — see the research addendum at
the bottom once the agent reports.)

**Decision:** honour release for the whole ascent. The decompiled constants are unchanged;
this is a fidelity bug in our reading of them, and `docs/research/hk-frame-data.md` gets a
clarifying line.
