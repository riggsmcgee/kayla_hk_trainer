# Playtest 6 — user notes (2026-08-26)

The first playtest of the playtest-5 build (branch `proactive/2026-08-26-1054`). Seven notes: one
defect in the gamepad shipped the same day, one written lesson, one new sandbox, one structural
change to how every enemy challenge is scored, and three notes that together describe the ending of
the whole project. Settled over three rounds of `/grilling` on 2026-08-26.

**Everything under "Ratified decisions" is a decision, not a suggestion — do not re-open it without
the user.** Where a decision strikes something an earlier round ratified, the struck line is quoted
so nobody has to guess whether the contradiction was noticed.

This round was scouted before a single question was asked: seven read-only agents over the source,
one per note, each report then attacked by a skeptic told to refute it. **Six of the seven scouts
came back partly wrong**, and the corrections are in "The arithmetic" below. That process is the
only reason the numbers in this document can be trusted, and one of the errors it caught was the
same class of error that reached a shipped test in playtest 5.

## The notes, verbatim

### 1. The gamepad works, but the game does not know she is using it

> "The gamepad seems to be working. Make sure that the game keeps track of which input method she's
> using, so keyboard or controller. Keep track of which button she's using for which things so that
> it can update on things like retry level, so it says the correct button. Right now I'm playing with
> the gamepad, but it still says to press X on the keyboard. The functionality works. I just press the
> tech button and it works, but it's not saying the right thing on the screen."

### 2. A written section on dashing

> "I want to add a written section that talks a little bit more about dashing. A useful skill to learn
> is knowing how to hit an opponent and then immediately dash away. We don't need to add any training
> or tools for this. I just want to have a written section on that."

### 3. A sandbox after the controller pick

> "After she picks her controller, let's throw her into a sandbox. No enemies, no challenges or
> anything. Ask her to move around and make sure she is able to move, attack in each direction, jump
> and dash. Also have the means on that screen to quickly change those controls, like in the settings,
> so that she can have a setup that she actually uses in-game."

### 4. Hits stop being a gate and become a score

> "For the dodge arena and really any enemy challenge, I don't think we should have her have to hit a
> goal of how many to get to go into X1. We just say you have to survive for X amount of time, and
> then your score is how many hits you land on enemies in that time. At the end of development, we can
> go through and play through everything and try to get a high score. We can then include that we can
> say, 'Riggs's high score is this. Can you beat him?' It shouldn't be mandatory to move on to the
> next level."

### 5. She has never seen the dog arrive

> "I think we should make Bill the dog's entrance unskippable and unfast-forwardable. I'm always
> already moving and pressing buttons whenever he enters the screen, so that means that it's skipped
> every time. I have not once actually been able to see his entrance screen."

### 6. The Bills bow

> "Along those lines, I want to add a special animation at the very end. Whenever she beats the bills,
> I think they should either applaud or bow down to her.
>
> This is another one where I think you could make a couple animations that I can look at. For those
> animations, it's actually a lot nicer for me if I can just see them in an artifact and look at them
> next to each other. I'm not having to go around and mess with the settings, so let's experiment some
> with that in the next iteration."

### 7. The ending

> "I also want to make an 8-bit version of myself and write a little monologue at the end, just saying
> congratulations to her. Maybe 30 seconds to a minute, something like that. The final thing can be an
> end screen where all of the enemies included come out and surround the player character, and they
> start cheering and confetti is falling. It's just like, 'Congratulations,' or something along those
> lines: 'You did it, you're the Hollow Knight Queen,' something like that."

And, in round 2, the shape of notes 6 and 7 together — **they are one sequence, not two**:

> "It'll just be after the time expires for the final boss. Instead of going to the next screen or
> showing retry, think of it narratively: both the bills now respect the character, and so they bow
> down to her. She just has to make it to that goal time without getting hit at all. If she does that,
> then both the bills become inactive. They have a little animation to end it all, then maybe the
> other enemies can run on screen. They celebrate in some way, shape, or form."

And, in round 3, how the cast celebrates — **by repurposing the attacks they already have**:

> "I think that we can actually repurpose the attacks you're talking about. What if the spitters shoot
> their attack just straight up in the air, and then, after just a moment, they explode into confetti?
> The warden is waving their shield back and forth in the duelist. His upward attack sort of looks
> like an applause. The crawler and the flyer maybe they can just sort of be there with party hats on."

## A live bug, found while scouting and confirmed in a browser

**The first time Kayla ever survives 1:30 against the Bills, the fight restarts from Bill's entrance
and the run is never recorded.** This was found by reading dependency arrays, and then watched
happening in Edge with an instrumented page. It is not a theory.

Frame-accurate timeline, milliseconds since page load:

```
97540   "1:29 / 1:30"
98540   "1:30 / 1:30"    "past 1:30 — how long can you go?"
98574   "0:00"           "Something is coming."      ← 34 ms later, two frames
100673  "BILL THE MAN"
```

The teardown, proven directly — `attachKeyboard`'s window listener is removed and re-added 15 ms
after the progress write:

```
98539  setItem  kayla-hk-dojo:progress  ..."finaleBossCleared":true...
98554  keyboard-detached
98556  keyboard-attached
```

**The chain.** `PlayWell.tsx`'s `onPassed` calls `markFinaleBossCleared()` then `refresh()`.
`progress.finaleBossCleared` flips false → true. That boolean is **dependency #8** of
`createSession`'s `useCallback`, and `createSession` is a dependency of `PracticeCanvas`'s effect
(`PracticeCanvas.tsx:61`). The effect tears down — `loop.stop()`, `detachKeyboard()` — and builds a
new session.

**Three things worse than the code alone suggested:**

- It does not restart into a running fight. It drops to `'intro'` then `'ready'` and **sits on "Move
  to begin"** — empty arena, both Bills gone, the dog gone, `29 hits ignored` reset to `0`.
- `kayla-hk-dojo:runs` is **`null`** at the end. Not "no boss run" — **no run of any kind was ever
  written.** `record()` only fires in the `'over'` branch, so her first 1:30 leaves no record and no
  best time.
- The ` / 1:30` goal silently vanishes from the HUD **forever**, because the new session is built
  with `cleared: true`.

**Why five playtests missed it:** a control run seeded with `finaleBossCleared: true` read
`1:29 → 1:30 → 1:31 → … → 1:43` with no restart at all. It fires **exactly once per browser
profile**. God mode is not the cause — it was only needed to survive that long; a real clear takes
the identical path. Zero console errors. Completely silent.

## The arithmetic the interview turned on

Six of the seven scouts were corrected by their skeptic. The corrections are marked, because the
correction is usually the more useful half.

### The dog's card is on screen for ONE simulation step

`pressedAnything` includes `input.left || input.right`, read as held-key **level** state. Step N
raises the card; step N+1 sees the direction she is already holding, calls `skipCard`, and the phase
flips to `'fighting'`.

**1/60 s = 16.7 ms = 0.67 % of its 2.5 s.** She has never seen it because it is not possible to see
it while moving.

### Bill the man's entrance is already unskippable, at 139 % of the fight

|                                                      | value                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Heavy entrance (thumps 1.2 + arrival 0.9 + card 0.7) | **2.8 s**                                                    |
| An idle Knight is caught at                          | **2.017 s** (measured against the real session)              |
| Entrance as a share of the fight                     | **139 %**                                                    |
| Ten minutes of that grind                            | ~**114** unskippable entrances, **zero** of which reach 0:30 |

**This is what decided note 5.** Unskippable is already affordable in this fight, demonstrated 114
times per ten minutes, without complaint. The dog's card costs **zero score** on top of that, because
the clock is paused during it. To lose one minute of session time to dog cards she must reach 0:30
twenty-four times, costing twelve minutes of surviving first.

### A hits GATE and a hits SCORE are an order of magnitude apart

Gates today: Colosseum **19** hits across five enemies (5+5+3+3+3), waves **16**.

**Corrected ceiling.** The scout said "~54 hits". Wrong twice, both in the same direction: the kill
cycle is `(hp−1) × 0.41 + RESPAWN_DELAY 1.4`, not `hp × 0.41 + 1.4` — the second cooldown runs
_inside_ the respawn wait — and it generalised from the walker, the slowest-scoring enemy.

| enemy                 | cycle             | ceiling in 60 s | vs. its gate        |
| --------------------- | ----------------- | --------------- | ------------------- |
| walker / flier (hp 2) | 1.81 s per 2 hits | **~66**         | ~13× the 5-hit gate |
| duelist (hp 4)        | 2.63 s per 4 hits | **~91**         | ~30× the 3-hit gate |

**5 is a floor a beginner clears; 66 is a ceiling nobody reaches.** A rival number has to sit
somewhere in between, and the project has already ratified that placing it wrong is a harm.

### There are 12 scoreable things in THREE currencies

4 pogo levels (fastest clear) + 5 Colosseum enemies (hits) + 2 waves (hits) + 1 boss = 12.
**7 hit counts, 5 times.** And a wave score is not the same currency as a Colosseum score: with four
bodies alive there is no 1.4 s empty-arena respawn gap at all, plus the 0:30 reinforcement doubling.

### Reviewing ONE candidate celebration in the running game costs 95.3 s of flawless play

2.8 s (Heavy entrance) + 2.5 s (the dog's card, unavoidable and clock-frozen) + 90 s = **95.3 s**.
Two candidates = **3 min 10.6 s**, and only one variant can be active at a time, so they can never be
seen side by side at any price. In an Artifact both loop simultaneously at **0 s** of play.

Both Bill painters are portable: **one type-only import each**, **six canvas API members total**
(`fillRect, fillStyle, save, restore, scale, translate`), 49,410 bytes of TypeScript, no
`enum`/`namespace`/`declare`. `esbuild` 0.28.2 is already installed.

### The dash buys exactly one extra tenth of a second — and it is already measured

From the shipped `attackers.test.ts`, against the real physics:

| she leaves after | running (332 px/s) | dashing (800 px/s) |
| ---------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| 0.00 s           | escapes            | escapes            |
| 0.10 s           | escapes            | escapes            |
| **0.15 s**       | **CAUGHT**         | escapes            |
| **0.20 s**       | **CAUGHT**         | escapes            |
| 0.30 s           | CAUGHT             | CAUGHT             |

The mechanism a speed comparison cannot supply, quoted from the same test: _"The column travels
forward at `antiAirDashSpeed` (260) while it is live, which is why a LATE run does not save her: she
only nets 72 px/s on it, and she needs ~80."_

The loop repeats every **0.600 s**, not every 0.417 s — `dashCooldown` is the bottleneck by
**0.183 s** of dead time per cycle. The dash covers **200 px in 15 frames** versus 83 px running: a
117 px head start at **2.41×** run speed.

### A 60 s monologue would be 21× the longest non-interactive beat in the game

60 / 2.8 = **21.4**. Even fully hurried at `INTRO_FAST_FORWARD` 2.5 it is **24 s**. The longest string
drawn on the canvas today is **76 characters**. This is why note 7's monologue became text boxes she
advances rather than a timed performance.

### An 8-bit human is 160 px tall and his whole face is 36 sub-cells

Master cell 8 px, nothing finer than 4 px, head = 3 cells = 24 px, 6.7 heads tall → **160 px**, which
is **3.33× the Knight**. Face = (24/4)² = **36 sub-cells**, of which Bill spends about five on his
entire expression: one eye, one mouth block, one nose block, one brow block.

**A likeness is not available at this resolution.** What is available is a silhouette plus one
oversized identifying prop — exactly how Bill works, whose 24 × 56 foam finger is 35 % of his height
and "is the read".

### The cast is 394 px of drawn ink, not 320

**A correction worth recording for its own sake.** The scout sized the ending's tableau from
`ENEMY_SIZES`, which is the **collision box**. The skeptic measured the actual drawn bounds:

| body    | collision box | measured ink   |
| ------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| flier   | 32            | **64** (wings) |
| spitter | 38            | **58**         |
| dog     | 64            | **68**         |
| bill    | 68 × 160      | **88 × 168**   |
| Knight  | 24 × 48       | **36 × 56**    |

Nine bodies leave **53.8 px per gap**, not 67. They still fit — but every published figure was ~25 %
optimistic, and **this is the same class of error the project was burned by in playtest 5: a hitbox
number reused as a visual number.**

## Ratified decisions

### The ending (notes 6 and 7, which are one feature)

- **1:30 is a FINISH LINE, not a checkpoint.** This **strikes** a ratified line:
  _"**1:30 marks the stop done**, and the fight keeps escalating after that for her best time."_
  (`PLAN.md:181`.) The reasoning that replaces it: an invisible score that runs past the ending is
  worse than an ending. Cheaper than it looks — `bestLine.ts` has **no boss line at all**, so the
  boss's "best time" is already invisible on the map sign. **The boss becomes pass/fail.**
- **The sequence, in order:**
  1. The clock reaches 1:30 → **both Bills go inactive and harmless**, the fight stops.
  2. The Bills' own animation — bow or applaud. _Portfolio; see below._
  3. The rest of the cast comes on and celebrates **using the attacks they already have**.
  4. Confetti, sourced from the spitters.
  5. 8-bit Riggs, text boxes, "You did it — you're the Hollow Knight Queen".
- **The cast celebrates by repurposing its own attack states.** The user's answer, and it is better
  than the alternative that was proposed to him:
  - **Spitter** — fires its volley straight up; the shots burst into confetti at the top.
  - **Warden** — waves its shield back and forth (`shieldDir` already flips `front`/`up` on a clock).
  - **Duelist** — its upward anti-air already reads as applause.
  - **Walker and flier** — present, in party hats.
- **This is what makes confetti affordable.** `juice.ts` has no emitter, no pool and no lifetimes —
  confetti was going to be a **new primitive** landing on `ComfortSettings.reduceFlashing`. Sourcing
  it from the spitters' volley reuses the projectile system she already reads.
- **The win phase turns damage resolution OFF entirely.** Driving real attack phases produces real
  hitboxes and real projectiles — a duelist in `antiair`/`active` is lethal. This is **not** a
  reopening of the immunity window struck in playtest 4 and again in playtest 5: the fight is
  **over**, not paused.
- **A new sixth `BossPhase`.** `BossPhase` is a closed union — `'intro' | 'ready' | 'fighting' |
'card' | 'over'` — and `'over'` is reachable only by being touched. **On a perfect run today the
  last frame of the entire dojo is a loss screen reading "Got you."** The alternatives were all
  rejected: celebrating under a live fight lets her die walking into a Bill that is congratulating
  her; pausing with the card phase is _"an interruption she did not ask for"_; and hanging it off the
  `'over'` screen attaches the applause to her death and never plays it on the run she remembers.
- **God mode does not earn the ending.** She is already required to reach 1:30 untouched by
  construction — one touch ends the run — but god mode took 29 hits and still reached 1:30 in the
  browser. Gate on zero **real** touches. A dev-drawer "play the ending" button exists so it can be
  watched during development without faking a win.
- **The monologue is WRITTEN, in text boxes she advances.** This keeps `PLAN.md`'s audio milestone
  shut: _"there is no `Audio`, no Web Audio and no sound file anywhere in this project… The moment
  sound exists it needs a mute in Comfort and a decision about what else gets it."_ A duration is how
  you measure speech; text boxes are paced by her, so "30 seconds to a minute" stops being a timer.
- **She advances every box with a fresh press**, through the same `createOverlayGate` playtest 5
  built. A 30–60 s non-interactive sequence is the wave auto-advance complaint at twenty times the
  scale, arriving at the moment she is most likely to be mashing.

### The animations ship as an ARTIFACT (note 6, second half)

- **Ratified process change.** `PLAN.md:63` already splits the portfolio medium — _"For a
  *behaviour* rather than a picture the portfolio is playable, not browsable"_ — and an animation is
  judged by **looking**. 95.3 s of flawless play per viewing settles it.
- **Compiled from the shipped `.ts` with the repo's own esbuild, and the generator script is kept in
  git.** Session 8's gallery sources were never committed, and its renderer says "Ported verbatim
  from the concept module" — a fork with nothing keeping it in sync.
- **Republish on every change.** `docs/skills-log.md`: _"A build that is not deployed is not
  delivered."_
- **Ask the near-miss question while the page is still open.** The last step of the portfolio process
  — measuring what the chosen designs share with the ones that nearly won — **has now failed to run
  twice**, both times because the user could not recall the near-misses. A live URL fixes the root
  cause.
- **Three candidates per artistic decision**, the project's own norm.

### The dog's entrance (note 5)

- **Unskippable, every time.** Not first-time-only: the persistence is real work (a new `ProgressV1`
  field, or deriving it from stored runs — which `trimRuns` can evict, and which **god-mode runs
  would silently consume**), and the replay-tax argument does not apply here.
- This **strikes** a playtest-4 line: _"**The dog's 0:30 card keeps its any-key skip AND gains the
  fast-forward.** It interrupts a run already in progress and she may be mid-panic, so the instant out
  stays. Two mechanisms on one overlay is untidy; the tidy version costs her a fight."_ The reasoning
  that replaces it: the clock is paused, so it costs her **zero score**, and Bill the man's entrance
  already proves an unskippable 2.8 s is affordable 114 times per ten minutes.
- **Strike the SKIP, not the fast-forward.** Removing the fast-forward alone changes nothing — the
  direction key skips first. Removing the skip alone still leaves the fast-forward, giving her 1.0 s.
- **The card copy's clause "your clock is paused" must survive.** It is the reassurance that makes an
  unskippable card acceptable.
- **Two unshipped defects this exposes, which must be fixed in the same change.** Nobody has ever
  seen this card, so nobody has seen either:
  1. **The dog has no walk pose during it.** `createEnemy` leaves `attackKind: null`, so
     `billDogPose` returns `'idle'` — **what an unskippable card reveals is the dog's idle pose
     sliding 280 px.** The fully-drawn `'walkIn'` trot was claimed by playtest 5's uncurl.
  2. **He may not be drawn at all.** `bringInTheDog` pins `prevDogFeet` to the off-screen start, and
     `prevDogFeet` is updated only in the fighting path — the card branch returns before it. Render
     lerps between them, so on a 60 Hz display he is drawn at his off-screen start for the entire
     card; on a faster display he oscillates, which is the interpolation `PLAN.md:59` forbids.

### Scoring (note 4)

- **Surviving the clock still CLEARS a stage. Hits become a displayed score.** This **strikes** a
  ratified line: _"**60 s everywhere + required clean hits to pass an arena stage**… Surviving alone
  was too easy ('just don't go near them'), so a stage is passed by surviving 60 s **and** landing 5
  clean hits on a dummy / 3 on an attacker"_ (`PLAN.md:50`), and it strikes playtest 5's
  _"**`hitsRequired` stays 6.**"_ one round later.
- **The worry that produced the rule is the user's own, and it is knowingly accepted back:** _"it's
  just so easy to avoid everything and just not go near the enemies… I basically don't use the Observe
  Mode at all."_ The answer had two clauses — (a) hits are required, and (b) every enemy hunts her.
  **Clause (b) survives and is now the only defence against corner-camping.** It has never been tested
  alone, because both shipped together.
- **`bests.ts` currently ranks backwards for this.** `if (a.cleared !== b.cleared) return a.cleared`
  puts a cleared 5-hit run above an uncleared 40-hit run — exactly wrong once hits are the score.
- **Rename "Skip this challenge", and stop the skip writing a permanent mark.** Two of the three
  plausible complaints behind this note are the **word** and the fact that skipping brands the map
  with a dashed-unfinished ring forever. This fixes most of the feeling without touching the stage
  rule.
- **The user asked for "not mandatory" four days ago and got the skip gate.** _"I don't think it
  should be mandatory but I do think it should be structured in a way where it's very clear that
  you're supposed to complete one topic before moving on."_ Note 4 is that request returning — which
  means the gate is not reading as optional to him.

### Riggs's high score (note 4, second half)

- **No rival score now.** Her own best only. The plumbing is shaped so one rival number is a one-line
  data add later.
- **The reason is already ratified:** _"**A best she cannot beat is worse than no best.**"_ With a
  ceiling 13–30× the old gate and twelve scoreable things in three currencies, a committed adult
  number is that principle's hardest test case.
- **If one is ever added, it goes on the pogo course.** Fastest clear is a single honest currency,
  she replays those most, and a time is something she can shave rather than a wall she bounces off.
  The boss can no longer host it — 1:30 is now a finish line, not a score.

### The input source (note 1)

- **The prompts follow the LAST-USED source.** It cannot be a mode: `mergeInput` ORs both frames
  every step and _"a source sitting idle contributes nothing, so plugging a pad in can never take the
  keyboard away"_ is ratified. Provenance is destroyed at exactly that point, so what is being added
  is a display signal, not a switch.
- **Name the ACTION where possible; the button POSITION where one must be named.** Precedent already
  ships — _"One touch ends it. Survive 1:30. Move to begin."_ Several of the ten wrong branches need
  no button name at all.
- **Letters are NOT adopted**, so `gamepad.ts`'s ban and the Settings copy that ships it both stand.
  Two facts killed the letters route: the site's own Setup lesson says _"Out of the box, jump (B) and
  attack (Y) sit under the same finger. **Remap once.**"_ — so after she follows it the letters are
  wrong — and **nobody has ever observed what her pads report.** A single Joy-Con is the canonical
  case browsers do _not_ fit to standard mapping.
- **The overlay label seam becomes a function called at draw time.** `OverlayControls.jumpKey` and
  `attackKey` are `string` today, baked in when the session is constructed. Making them `() => string`
  is the only shape that keeps copy live **without rebuilding the session — which restarts her run** —
  and keeps "no session learns what a gamepad is" true.
- **Rename the ratified rule.** `PLAN.md:57` and playtest 3 both call it _"Z = forward, X = again"_.
  Restate as **"jump = forward, attack = again"** in the same change, or the docs teach the next agent
  to hard-code Z and X.
- **The canvas caption is fixed regardless.** `PracticeCanvas` renders keyboard-only copy —
  _"Move with ←/→ or A/D · jump with Z or Space · slash with X or J · dash with C, K or Shift"_ — on
  all three mini-game pages, on screen the whole time she plays with a pad. **It is the single
  most-visible wrong string and the note does not mention it.**

### The sandbox (note 3)

- **Its own route, not `/lessons/setup`.** A `PracticeCanvas` there kills arrow-key and spacebar
  scrolling on the dojo's longest text page, breaking the ratified _"lesson pages must scroll and read
  like normal pages"_.
- **It counts as a VISIT, not a DONE.** Setup still finishes on answering the controller question, so
  this does not strike _"The answer is recorded and shown; **it changes nothing else**"_ outright —
  it adds a stop she can visit without changing what completes it.
- **The checklist must report WHICH SOURCE ticked each box.** This is the note's real value and
  without it the feature cannot do its job: `mergeInput` ORs the keyboard and pad frames before any
  session sees them, so **every box ticks identically whether she used the leverless or tapped Z.**
  Reporting the source turns the sandbox into **M7's outstanding human acceptance harness** instead of
  a checklist that proves nothing about her board.
- **"Attack in each direction" has a trap.** `nailDir` is `input.down && !p.grounded ? 'down' : …` —
  **down is gated on being airborne.** On the ground, holding down + attack silently produces a SIDE
  slash. Worse, `grounded` there is last step's value, so **jump + down + attack on the same frame
  also yields a side slash.** A twelve-year-old holding down and mashing attack on the floor will
  conclude the button is broken — **the checklist item she tries hardest is the one that cannot
  tick.** The copy must tell her to jump first.
- **Reuse the shipped pattern for controls beside a live canvas**: `PlayPogo` already renders
  `LevelPicker` chips above a keyed `PracticeCanvas`, with the focus dance documented for exactly
  this. While a capture is armed, stop feeding the merged frame to the session — otherwise the key
  binds _and_ jumps the Knight on the same frame.

### The dashing section (note 2)

- **It lives in LessonPogo, as an `<h2>` — not a seventh stop.** `chapters.test.ts` pins the exact
  six ids, `DojoMap` is a **typed** `Record<ChapterId, …>` (a new id is a compile error until art
  coordinates exist), and the map's road is five hand-authored SVG bezier legs. LessonPogo is ungated
  and its `.thesis` slot is unspent; Reading Enemies' is already spent.
- **It teaches the measured numbers, pinned against the engine constants by a test.** The thesis is
  not "the dash is fast" — it is **"the dash doubles your reaction window from a tenth of a second to
  two tenths, and after that you are committed."** There is precedent for pinning copy against
  constants, and this project has twice been burned by an unverified number in prose.
- **This knowingly relaxes a ratified line:** _"'Fewer words' applies to the lesson pages too: trim
  ledes, lean on the demos and diagrams, keep the warm voice but at half the length."_ The user struck
  it deliberately: _"it's okay that we're adding a little bit… try to keep it more on the brief side.
  Don't ramble."_
- **It must not teach dash-cancelling.** At her kit the nail cooldown (0.41 s) exceeds the swing
  duration (0.35 s), so cancelling changes nothing — the flat cadence is faithful.
- **One correction to the note's premise, worth putting in the copy:** against Bill the man's lance,
  dashing away is the **wrong** answer and the ratified one is the opposite. His hot lance at 950 px/s
  outruns her 800 px/s dash outright, and _"she must be airborne."_

## Hidden work — none of it is optional, none of it is in the notes

- **`useBindings` and `useGamepadBindings` are NOT a shared store.** Both are plain per-component
  `useState` with a lazy localStorage read, so **every call site gets its own copy**. A rebind on
  Settings does not reach a mounted `PracticeCanvas` at all — it reaches localStorage and is picked up
  on the next mount. **Note 3's "quick rebinding on the same screen" would update the rows and not the
  Knight.** There are three consumers, not two: the pages' label strings, the canvas's keyboard
  adapter, and the session's baked-in overlay copy — which only refreshes on the very remount that
  must be suppressed. Migrating to the `useSyncExternalStore` pattern touches four pages plus
  Settings. **This is the largest unbudgeted item in the round and it is a prerequisite for both note
  1 and note 3.**
