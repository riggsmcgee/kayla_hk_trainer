## What the interview changed

**The copy deck failed twice, for two different reasons, and the second correction was his.** The
first attempt was an inventory organised by CODE FILE, with entries called `summonFirst` and
`hudNeverTouched`, each carrying the source's own reasoning about why it was worded that way — a
developer's document handed to someone who asked to edit his website. Told that was wrong, the
interview's first instinct was to design something MORE elaborate: curated cards, one per moment,
real rendered game frames, a taxonomy of which beats have words. He cut through all of it in one
sentence: **"I just want a one-to-one recreation of the site that I can edit the text in easily."**
The right answer was simpler than the thing that had already failed, not more sophisticated than it.
**When a deliverable misses, check whether the fix is a simplification before designing a better
miss.**

**He overturned his own ratified ruling, deliberately, once shown it.** Playtest 5 deleted the wave
stage-clear auto-advance **outright** — "it is the only one that needs no input" — and playtest 6
rejected a long non-interactive sequence as "the wave auto-advance complaint at twenty times the
length." Text that arrives on its own is the same shape. Put to him as his own precedent, he kept the
new decision and took the interruptible form, which is what stops it repeating the original failure:
nothing on this page can strand her, because forward always completes the sentence she is reading.

**He turned down the rescue of his own idea, again.** Offered a real curtain call — every character
DRAWN and walking on, rather than a list of names — he took the deletion instead. It is the same move
he made in playtest 7, where the scarier version of the fake-out was a wave card naming the enemies
and he took the silence. **Twice now the smaller answer has been his, and twice it has been right.**

**Candidate B was already speaking before anyone asked it to.** The mouth animation in note 3 turned
out to exist: the drawing carries a `mouthOpen` state with an upper lip and a dark gap under it, on a
documented SPEECH clock where "the brow lifts 8 px and the mouth opens" on the second of two nods. It
is currently driven by the wall clock. The work is to drive it from "is text typing" instead — a
wiring change, not new art.

**The controller hole is real and eight sessions old.** `progress.controller` is WRITTEN when she
picks in chapter one and READ in exactly two places: to print "LEVERLESS it is. Stick with it." and to
mark the chapter done. **It configures nothing.** Meanwhile a single `DEFAULT_GAMEPAD_BINDINGS`
serves both controllers, and the leverless diagram's own accessible description says in so many
words: _"Attack (Y) and Jump (B) sit under the same finger until remapped."_ **The site tells her
there is a clash and then does nothing about it.**

**"No bounce target, just check she can attack in all directions" verifies the pogo for free.** In
`player.ts`, `nailDir` is `'down'` only when `input.down && !p.grounded` — the downslash is an
AIRBORNE-ONLY input. So a checklist item for the downslash proves she can perform the compound mid-air
press the pogo is built on, without the sandbox needing anything to bounce off. His instinct arrived
at the right answer by a shorter route than the design would have.

## The arithmetic

### Talking pace is 12 characters per second

Comfortable adult reading is roughly 12–16 cps; conversational speech sits near 12. The four existing
messages average about **150 characters**, so each takes about **12 s**, and the whole letter runs
about **60 s** including gaps. That is the number to watch when the longer version is written: at 12
cps, every 150 characters added costs another twelve seconds of her sitting still.

### A one-to-one site fits in an artifact with room to spare

| thing              | size          |
| ------------------ | ------------- |
| the built app, JS  | **414.88 kB** |
| the built app, CSS | **27.42 kB**  |
| artifact budget    | **16 MB**     |
| what that leaves   | ~97% unused   |

So "publish the actual site" is not a stretch — it is **2.7% of the budget**. The obstacle was never
size; it was that canvas text is pixels, which is the same wall that killed the in-site editor in
playtest 7 and which the string extraction is what gets around.

### The extraction left is the same size as the extraction done

| where                             | count                   |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| canvas strings, already extracted | **52**                  |
| short JSX text nodes remaining    | **~52**                 |
| page and component files          | **23**                  |
| long prose blocks (stay in place) | few, ratified read-only |

Roughly one focused pass, not the 200-string marathon the last contract implied. The lesson prose is
excluded by playtest 7's ruling and stays where it is.

### At a 16 px cell, about five features carry the likeness

Hair shape and colour, glasses or none, facial hair or none, face width, one expression. A photograph
does not change that arithmetic — it just makes those five choices correct instead of invented.

## Ratified decisions

### Riggs (note 1)

- **Candidate B wins. A and C are deleted.** The portfolio rule this project runs on is that choosing
  one deletes the other two, and the last gallery drifted out of sync precisely because losers were
  kept alive.
- **B's STYLE is locked** — the glasses, the three-tone ramps, the detail level, the 16 px cell. The
  photo changes the FACE INSIDE it, not the drawing around it.
- **One round-trip on the likeness.** He sends the photo, one revised drawing comes back, he reacts to
  a picture. Not a paragraph about a picture: the celebration poses and the bow tie both went faster
  that way.
- **The painter's signature is free to change** now that A and C are gone, which is what lets the
  mouth take a `speaking` flag.

### The credits (note 2)

- **Deleted outright.** Not expanded to a curtain call, not extended to more characters. The cast
  entries come out of `copy/theEnd.ts` with them.
- **`#/the-end` ends on his face and his last sentence**, with a quiet way back to the map underneath.
  Nothing follows it, because anything that follows it competes with the thing he actually wrote.
- **Settings' "Watch the credits" becomes "Read it again"** and points at the same page. The letter is
  now the whole thing.

### The read-off (note 3)

- **Typewriter, character by character.** The site has no audio, so the rhythm of the letters is the
  only voice available — the same reasoning that made Bill's barking drawn rather than heard.
- **12 characters per second, 1.2 s between messages.**
- **Automatic, but interruptible.** Forward **completes the current message instantly** rather than
  skipping it. This is the clause that keeps it from repeating the wave auto-advance failure: a reader
  who looks away for five seconds never loses a sentence, and an impatient one never has to wait.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` shows each message whole** instead of typing it. The mouth still moves
  for the message's duration in both modes.
- **The mouth moves only while characters are appearing.** He has finished a sentence; his mouth
  should close. **The blink and the nod continue through the gaps**, because a completely frozen face
  reads as a crash — the same reason everything keeps its idle animation through the ending's
  silences.
- **The longer text is written OUT LOUD in the next chat**, then shaped. The four that exist came from
  him talking and are the best-sounding writing in the project; a drafted extension would be an
  imitation of them.

### The copy tool (note 4)

- **A one-to-one recreation of the site, published as an artifact.** Navigable, playable, real. Not a
  wireframe, not cards, not a list.
- **Click any text, type over it, save.** The artifact saves its own new versions, so a pass done
  across several sittings survives without anything being sent.
- **Canvas text is the one exception and it cannot be clicked** — it is pixels, not elements. A live
  panel lists the strings for whichever screen he is looking at, editable there, with the canvas
  redrawing as he types. He still edits everything; he just does not click the pixels.
- **The ~52 remaining short strings are extracted FIRST.** A tool where half the words are greyed out
  is the same failure in a different costume. The extraction is also load-bearing under the cheap
  fallback — if he ends up dictating changes in chat instead, they land in one module rather than
  across 23 files.
- **Long lesson prose stays in its pages and is shown READ-ONLY.** Playtest 7's ruling stands: a
  paragraph with three links in it is worse as a text box. He sees it in context and says what he
  wants changed.

### The controller (note 5)

- **Picking a controller must DO something.** Preset then offer: the choice immediately applies a
  layout that fits that controller — moving Attack off the same finger as Jump on the leverless — and
  then one line offers the four-button capture so her real hardware wins if she takes it.
- **Presets are positions, never letters.** Pads disagree about which letter sits where; this is
  already ratified and a preset does not get to break it.
- **A sandbox in Setup: a bare floor and her Knight.** Nothing to bounce off, nothing to fight,
  no pass and no fail. It is the one place on the site with neither.
- **A checklist gate, and it is a hard one — "just a little hard gate".** She proves the controls work
  AND learns what she is able to do. Seven items, derived from the kit in PLAN §5 and from
  `player.ts`: move left, move right, jump, dash, slash sideways, slash up, slash down. **The
  downslash can only be performed in the air**, so the last item proves the compound press the pogo
  needs without a bounce target existing.
- **She can still skip it**, like every gate on the road. Nothing ever traps her.

## Hidden work — none of it is in the notes, none of it is optional

- **The read-off's pacing needs its own clock, and it must not be the render clock.** The typewriter's
  cursor advances on simulated time; a `requestAnimationFrame` delta drives it, but the character
  count has to be derived from elapsed seconds rather than incremented per frame, or the letter reads
  at a different speed on a 144 Hz monitor than on a 60 Hz one. Same class of bug as the ending's rise
  (`cda951e`, and the `alpha` note in playtest 7's hidden work).
- **`theEndCopy.cast`, `castHeading`, `creditsHeading`, `builtHeading` and `builtLine` all die with
  the credits**, and so do the tests that assert the roll — including the one that derives the cast
  from `ROSTER`. Deleting a feature means deleting its tests, not leaving them asserting a screen
  nobody can reach.
- **The forward key's job on `#/the-end` changes meaning.** Today it ADVANCES; after this it
  COMPLETES, and only advances once the message is finished. The existing tests press it once per
  message and will need to press it twice or wait.
- **A `speaking` flag has to reach `paintRiggsB` from the page**, which means the painter's signature
  gains a parameter and `paintRiggs`'s dispatcher gains it too. Safe now that A and C are deleted;
  it would have meant editing three files a week ago.
- **The one-to-one artifact runs the REAL app, which means it runs the real localStorage.** Editing
  the site inside an artifact will write progress and settings into that artifact's own origin — which
  is isolated per artifact, so it cannot corrupt her real progress, but it does mean the editor opens
  with a blank save every time and every gated screen is locked. **The editor needs a seeded state**
  or half the site is unreachable inside it.
- **A checklist gate in Setup needs somewhere to live in `ProgressV1`.** `controller` is already there;
  the sandbox's seven ticks are not, and `chapterGate`/`chapterLocked` in `storage/progress.ts`
  currently derive Setup's completion from `progress.controller !== undefined` alone.
- **`DEFAULT_GAMEPAD_BINDINGS` becomes a function of the controller choice**, which means the Settings
  page's "reset to defaults" for the controller has to reset to HER controller's defaults, not the
  generic set. That button was just relabelled this sprint; it now also changes behaviour.

## Open, and deliberately not decided here

- ~~**Her leverless may report as a KEYBOARD, not a gamepad.**~~ **ANSWERED 2026-08-27, after the
  sprint:** _"this registers as a gamepad. It's a controller, not as a keyboard. I've tested this
  before. I know this is the case."_ So the gamepad path DOES run, nothing collapses, and item 6 is
  the full job in `engine/gamepad.ts` — `DEFAULT_GAMEPAD_BINDINGS` is the table a preset rewrites.
  Four sessions of PLAN calling this "ten seconds to find out" ended in one sentence from the only
  person who could answer it.
  **What is still not known** is the board's BUTTON INDEX MAP. "It enumerates as a gamepad" and "we
  know which index her Attack sits on" are different facts, and only the first one has been
  established. This is exactly why the ratified decision is _preset THEN offer_: the preset is a
  good guess at the positions, and the four-button capture is there so her real hardware overrules
  it. Build the capture offer as a first-class path, not a fallback nobody takes.
- **How long the longer read-off should run.** At 12 cps every 150 characters costs twelve more
  seconds. The four current messages take about a minute; eight would take two. Nobody has decided
  where the ceiling is, and it will be obvious when the text exists.
- **What the editor does about screens that need progress to reach.** Seeding a full save is the
  obvious answer, but "the site as she will actually see it" and "every screen reachable for editing"
  are not the same artifact, and the difference has not been thought through.
