# Playtest 4 — user notes (2026-08-25)

The first playtest of the Two Bills, played on the sprint branch
`proactive/2026-08-25-1527`. Four notes, all about the bottom of the well, plus the finale's
waves. Settled over four rounds of `/grilling` on 2026-08-25; **everything under "Ratified
decisions" is a decision, not a suggestion — do not re-open it without the user.**

---

## The notes, verbatim

### 1. The dog's bones should be thrown, not fired

> "I envisioned Bill the dog's projectiles differently. I thought that he would actually shoot the
> bones, and they would sort of spin around and go around the map and actually bounce them."

Today they are the spitter's fan exactly — `fanShots`, straight flight, dead on contact with any
solid.

### 2. The dog's roll should be more aggressive

> "Speaking of bouncing, can we make Bill the dog's bounce attack a bit more aggressive? A little
> bit faster pace, maybe higher bounces, so you can't pogo over it. I guess, depending on where
> he's at in the bounce, if he's coming at you, you might have to run under. That might be the only
> correct solution."

And, in round 2, the mechanic that came out of it:

> "The dog ball isn't really meant to be pogoed off of. It should bounce up and down quickly. We
> could actually do it to where, if she hits it from below, though, so she stands there and she
> pogos up, she can bounce it back up. The ball won't actually hit her, so she can bounce the dog
> and keep him in the air. That would actually be really fun."

### 3. The finale's waves have no difficulty jump

> "For the waves on the final gauntlet, on the waves leading up to the Bill fight, I'm not seeing
> the jump in difficulty. I believe after 30 seconds, there's supposed to be up to four enemies
> that spawn. Can you go ahead and make that happen?"

**Cause, found before the interview: the feature was never built.** T10 of
`docs/plans/2026-08-24-playtest-3-build.md` specced reinforcements in session 7 and no session ever
implemented it. The code still ships three waves of two enemies with no arrivals at all. He is not
misreading a weak curve — there is no curve.

### 4. Both Bills need an entrance

> "I want both builds to have a 2- to 3-second animation where they enter the battle. It's where
> their intro is, and it tells their name and has a custom animation."

And the direction, in round 2:

> "Let's build a little bit of suspense into the bill reveal. Maybe whenever it opens, it's just
> the player character on screen, and then there are big thumbs \[thumps], like footsteps. Then
> Bill comes onto the screen in an interesting way. When Bill the dog comes on, maybe Bill the man
> looks winded and calls for help, and you hear barking. Then the dog comes on and does something
> cute or fun."

## Ratified decisions (four rounds of `/grilling`, 2026-08-25)

### The dog's bones (note 1)

- **They bounce, off every surface**, and spin as they go.
- **A bounce budget of three, spent by ANY surface** — floor, wall or ceiling. A bone that hits
  wall → ceiling → floor is done. _(The user overrode the recommendation of floor-only; the budget
  is the readable limit and it is meant to be short.)_ The number is a knob if they die too fast.
- **A ceiling is added, and it is BOSS-ONLY.** `arenaWorld()` is shared with the Colosseum and the
  waves and is pinned at exactly 3 solids (`enemies.test.ts:245`); adding a ceiling there would
  also change the spitter, whose shots currently leave the top of the screen and die. The boss gets
  its own world instead, so no mode she has already playtested changes.
- **They stay nail-pokeable.** That link is the one that matters — it is what makes them fair, and
  it is the thing chapter 3 taught.
- **This deliberately breaks "nothing new is taught at the end of the road."** Ratified, with the
  reasoning: bouncing is the one thing this entire dojo has taught her, so a bone that arcs and
  rebounds is _more_ the dojo's language, not less. The boss is already the one place the plan lets
  a rule bend (the palette). **Record it in `PLAN.md` §3 beside the pixel-art decision** rather
  than leaving the old "a 3-shot spread like the spitter's" line standing.
  > _"Let's break the rule for this instance. Give her a special challenge right at the end."_

### The dog's roll (note 2)

- **The pogo-safe cap is GONE.** `rollSafeCap`, `enemyHurtsBox`'s dog branch and the pale cap drawn
  in `renderBills.ts` all come out. This **strikes a ratified line** from playtest 3 — _"pogoable
  on top, damaging on the sides, deliberately the red-orb vocabulary from level 2"_ — and
  `PLAN.md` §5 mode 3 must be corrected, not just added to.
- **The ball is ALWAYS lethal on body contact**, rising or falling, no exceptions and no immunity
  window. _(This overrode a proposed "harmless while rising" rule.)_
- **The ball is not a pogo surface.** A downslash onto it must not bounce her — she simply dies.
  `applyPogoBounce` currently fires on nail contact with _every_ enemy; the rolling dog needs to be
  the exception.
- **She can already run under it** and nobody knew: at the current apex the ball's underside sits
  81 px above her head and she is 47 px tall. Removing the cap is the whole fix — "under" only
  ever failed to occur to anyone because pogoing the top was free.
- **Higher is not more aggressive.** A taller arc gives her a _bigger_ tunnel to walk through. The
  knobs that raise pressure are horizontal speed and the gap between rolls.

### The volley — the new mechanic (note 2, round 2)

The ball can be **hit from below with the up-slash** and sent back up, so she can rally it and keep
the dog airborne. Nothing else in the dojo has ever rewarded the up-slash.

- **She must connect BEFORE it reaches her.** Her up-nail covers a band 48–128 px above her head;
  her hurtbox starts at 47. There is a strictly-safe strip about 81 px deep. **In the 57 px strip
  where the ball overlaps both her nail band and her body, connecting does NOT save her** — it has
  already reached her. Ratified deliberately: the target is the strip _above_ her, not a swat off
  her own face.
- **The rally preserves his horizontal speed**, so it drifts across the arena and she has to chase
  it. Straight-up would make it a stationary minigame.
- **The 5 s roll timer keeps running** during a rally, so it delays where he lands but can never
  become a stall.
- **What stops it being a free win: Bill the man.** Volleying means standing on the floor, and the
  floor is where the lance lives. No artificial cap — a boss that refuses to play along is the
  fight telling her to stop having fun. **Escalation is seasoning**: each return comes back a
  little faster, so a long rally feels like it is getting away from her.
- **She is never told about it.** No copy, no tutorial, no hint in the intro.
  > _"I don't think we have to specifically tell her about the volley. It's something that she can
  > beat the entire fight without doing, but if she figures it out, she'll feel super smart."_
- **Therefore the roll MUST be survivable without volleying** — by position and by running under
  the high phase. The volley is a flourish, never the answer.

### The arc shape — a five-variant portfolio (note 2)

> _"I think this is a situation where I just need to try out five different examples and then go
> off that."_

- **Build five roll behaviours for the user to choose between**, the way the nine-design Bill
  portfolio worked in session 8.
- **Every variant must alternate**, so each one keeps a low phase the volley can live on. A tall
  arc that skitters low every third hop still has a rally window; it just makes her wait for it.
- **The volley window is the binding constraint, and it is tight.** Time the ball spends in the
  safe strip, by apex:

  | apex           | speed entering the strip | time to cross it | her nail is live 0.15 s |
  | -------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------- | ----------------------- |
  | 128 px (today) | 0 px/s                   | 0.33 s           | comfortable             |
  | 203 px         | 474 px/s                 | 0.15 s           | exactly one nail window |
  | 295 px         | 708 px/s                 | 0.11 s           | needs ~0.04 s precision |

  **A towering arc deletes the volley.** Cap the apex around 150 px and put the variation into
  speed, cadence and rhythm.

### The finale's waves (note 3)

Build **T10 as specced** in `docs/plans/2026-08-24-playtest-3-build.md`, with one change:

- **Two waves, not three.** `FINALE_WAVE_COUNT` becomes 2 and must be _derived_ from
  `FINALE_WAVES.length` so it can never drift again.
- **All reinforcements arrive at 0:30, not staggered.** _(This is the one deviation from T10, which
  had +1 at 0:30 and +1 at 0:45.)_ Two enemies become **four** at thirty seconds.
  > _"No, let's actually just do two waves. The enemies increase from two to four at 30 seconds."_
- Wave 1 "The pests" — walker + flier, joined by walker + flier. **10 hits.**
- Wave 2 "The real ones" — duelist + spitter, joined by **warden** + duelist. **6 hits.**
- **Hits and the 60 s do not grow.** Four bodies is four targets, so hits get _easier_ to land;
  danger is the thing that grew.
- **Migration is generous**: old waves 1 and 2 are credited as the new 1 and 2, old wave 3 is
  dropped silently. Anyone who had cleared the finale keeps it cleared.
- Everything else in T10 still stands and is not re-decided here — in particular the respawn bug
  (`def.enemies[slot]` never returns a dead _reinforcement_), `joinX` instead of extending
  `spawnX`, driving the schedule off `stage.elapsed` rather than `simTime`, and twin de-syncing.

### The entrances (note 4)

- **2–3 seconds, both Bills, name plus a custom animation.**
- **The clock is frozen during the intro**, then the ready prompt appears and **her first input
  starts the fight** — preserving both ratified rules (Bill's clock starts on her own input; an
  interruption she did not ask for costs her nothing). A slow intro can never eat her best time.
- **It replays on every retry.** Holding the jump key does **not** skip it — it runs the intro at
  **2–3× speed**.
  > _"Let's do it where if you hold down the jump button, the intro isn't skipped, but it goes at
  > two or three times speed, something like that."_
- **The dog's 0:30 card keeps its any-key skip AND gains the fast-forward.** It interrupts a run
  already in progress and she may be mid-panic, so the instant out stays. Two mechanisms on one
  overlay is untidy; the tidy version costs her a fight.
- **Bill the man is NOT on screen when the beat opens** (he stands at x = 868 today). The arena
  opens empty but for the Knight; **3–4 thumps with screen-shake land while he is still out of
  frame**; then he enters **from the right**, the far side from her spawn — the same side the dog
  already walks in from, so the well keeps a consistent "they come from over there" geography.
- **The dog's entrance**: Bill the man looks winded and calls for help; barking arrives from
  off-screen; the dog bursts in and does something cute.
- **There is no audio in this project** — no `Audio`, no Web Audio, no sound files anywhere. The
  barking is **visual**: Bill shouts in his own pixel lettering, "WOOF!" arrives from off-screen
  with motion lines. **Audio becomes its own milestone in `PLAN.md` §8**, because the moment sound
  exists it needs a mute in Comfort and a decision about what else gets it.
- **The animations must stay stepped.** `PLAN.md` §3 ratifies that nothing in either Bill module
  interpolates — every offset is a whole 4 px step on a `Math.floor(t * hz)` clock. That was the
  single axis separating the two designs the user picked from the seven they did not. **The
  entrances are held to the same rule.**

### How the art gets decided

> _"With a lot of the artistic decisions, let's do what you did with the character design. Have
> multiple different variations for different possibilities of directions that we can go, or ways
> it can be animated, and then I'll pick my favorite from the options."_

**Every artistic decision in this round ships as a portfolio, not a pick.** At minimum: Bill's
entrance, the dog's entrance, the bone's tumble, the ball's new look (the pale cap is gone and
needs replacing — the hazard orb's dark ring is the site's existing "bounce off this and it still
hurts" marker), and the five roll behaviours. Session 8's process is the template: generate a
diverse set, publish it as one browsable page, let the user choose, then **measure what the chosen
ones have in common** — that is how the "must not be smoothed" rule was found.

## Priority for the next session

In the user's order of raising, which is also the sensible build order:

1. **The waves (T10)** — the only note that is a missing _feature_ rather than a redesign, it is
   fully specced already, and it is the one thing standing between her and a finale that escalates.
2. **The roll: cap out, always lethal, not a pogo surface** — small, and it unblocks the volley.
3. **The volley** — the new mechanic, and the one the user is most excited about.
4. **The bones: ceiling, bouncing, spin, budget of three.**
5. **The entrances**, last, because they are the largest art surface and the portfolio needs the
   fight to be settled before it is worth animating.

## Open, and deliberately not decided here

- **The five roll variants are a taste call**, not an engineering one. Build them, publish them,
  let the user pick.
- **Whether the ball's new look invites the volley.** The user ruled out _telling_ her; whether the
  ball is _drawn_ with a strikeable-looking underside is answered by whichever art variant wins.
- **The boss's first tuning pass still has not happened** (`PLAN.md` §8). Every number in the Bills
  is derived, not played. This round changes several of them; it does not replace that pass.
