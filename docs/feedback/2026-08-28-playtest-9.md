# Playtest 9 — the practice floor

**2026-08-28.** One annotated screenshot of the Setup sandbox and two sentences, delivered at the
top of an unattended sprint. No interview: he wrote the note and left. Everything below the fold is
therefore the sprint's reading of it, marked as such, rather than anything he ratified in the room.

---

## How the sprint read it, and the reading it did not take

**"And it's not working" is the SITUATION the button is for, not a bug report.** She presses Jump,
nothing happens, and the fix is on the row instead of two pages away in Settings. The other
reading — that the checklist itself is broken — was rejected on evidence: Session 17 drove it end to
end in a browser and watched the counter fall 7 → 1, and nothing has touched it since. If that
reading is wrong it is a small change and not a rebuild.

**The sentence names one row and one button.** "It should say 'Jump' and next it would be 'Remap'."
So the unit is the checklist row, not a panel, not a link to Settings, and not a mode the page
enters. What that costs is spelled out under "the two-control rows" below.

## What was decided without him, and why each was decidable

Two calls, both stated at the top of the handoff rather than buried, and both cheap to reverse.

### The floor is a PAGE, not a seventh stop on the road

He said "its own page". The question that leaves open is whether it becomes a stop — the road's own
shape is `learn → prove → practice → next`, and a lesson that teaches followed by a floor that
proves is exactly that shape. So the case for a seventh stop is real.

It was decided against on cost, and the cost is measurable rather than felt:

| what a seventh stop would take | evidence                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| re-author two bezier legs      | all five `LEGS` in `DojoMap.tsx` start and end at hand-typed ±36 offsets from a stop centre; nothing computes that 36    |
| re-author every stratum below  | each `band-N` top curve is a character-for-character copy of the band above it                                           |
| a sixth glyph                  | six hand-inked 32×32 icons, and the `Glyph` switch does NOT fail to compile for a missing one — it renders an empty disc |
| a taller map                   | `H = 1080` and a matching `aspect-ratio` in `map-states.css`                                                             |
| the strip's single row         | `gates.css` says in prose that six fit one row; a seventh wraps, undoing playtest 3 note 14                              |

The last precedent agrees: the commit that took the road from five stops to six was a ratified
playtest response touching four files by about five hundred lines. **A stop is a redraw and a design
conversation. A page is a route.** So the floor sits between Setup and Pogo in the forward chain
without being numbered: Setup's button reads "Next: Try it out", and the floor carries the
"Next: Pogo" that Setup used to.

**What this costs, and it is not nothing:** the map never points at the floor. Its sign names the
first unfinished CHAPTER, and the floor is not one. Every route to it runs through Setup.

### The gate ships, and existing saves are grandfathered

Playtest 8 asked for "just a little hard gate" and the sandbox that feeds it, and Session 17 built
everything except the gate — deliberately, because requiring the seven ticks would un-complete
chapter 1 for every save that already existed, Kayla's included. That was left as a decision.

**"Finish off everything on the handoff that you can" plus a migration that costs nobody anything is
enough to decide it.** The reason it was a decision was the risk, and grandfathering removes the
risk; the code is a few lines either way and the alternative is one line.

The migration is the part that needed care, and the first design of it was wrong:

- **Inferring "grandfathered" from a missing `setupChecks` key does not survive contact with the
  page.** `markSetupChecks(['left'])` turns `undefined` into `['left']`, so chapter 1 would
  un-complete the moment she walked left on the floor out of curiosity — revoked by the exact action
  the floor is designed to invite.
- **What ships instead MATERIALISES the seven.** A save that answered the controller before the
  sheet existed reads back with all seven ticked, so every later write is idempotent: adding 'left'
  changes nothing, and re-picking the same board preserves it.
- **From here on, answering the controller seeds an EMPTY sheet**, which is what makes a new save
  gated for real. An absent sheet means grandfathered; an empty one means gated. Six tests hold that
  distinction, and one of them exists only to stop a future "drop empty arrays to keep the blob
  small" tidy-up from silently promoting every gated player.
- **What the rule cannot distinguish** is a save from before the floor existed and a save made after
  it that never opened it. That is deliberate: the promise is "nothing already complete becomes
  incomplete", and both of those are already complete.

Proved in a browser rather than asserted: a save with a controller and no sheet reaches Greenpath on
the map, opens Pogo, and **is still open after walking left on the floor.**

## The two-control rows, which the note does not cover

Five of the seven items need one control. Two need two: "Slash up" is a direction AND a swing, and
so is "Slash down". A single Remap on those rows would rebind the wrong half half the time.

They get a line each, direction first, because the direction is almost always the one that is wrong
— Attack has already been proved by the sideways slash two rows above.

## The capture listens to both hands at once

Settings asks the narrow question on purpose: its Controls section is the keyboard's and its
Controller section is the pad's. The floor asks the wide one — the first key or button she presses
wins — and that is not a convenience. **Her leverless enumerates as a gamepad whose button index map
nobody has ever established.** "Press the one you mean" is the only thing that can be right about
hardware nobody has measured, and it is the same reasoning that made the four-button capture a
first-class path in playtest 8 rather than a fallback.

## Hidden work — none of it is in the note, none of it was optional

- **A rebind used to restart the game.** `PracticeCanvas` hung its session and its input adapters
  off one effect whose dependencies included the bindings, so changing a key tore the session down
  and built a new one. Invisible on Settings, which has no Knight; on a floor where she is standing
  testing her controller it is the Knight vanishing the moment she fixes a button. The session and
  the adapters have separate lives now.
- **A capture must not also be played.** `inputPaused` hands the session an empty frame while a
  capture is open, and still SAMPLES the real frame and throws it away — `sample()` clears the press
  edges, so draining is what stops the jump she pressed during a capture from firing the instant it
  closes. The pad is why this exists at all: the keyboard adapter already ignores keys pressed while
  a button has focus, and nothing anywhere ignores a gamepad.
- **The chapter's `done` line was a lie the moment the gate shipped.** "Answer one question — which
  controller?" is printed on the map's sign and on every gate panel. It now reads "Pick a
  controller, then prove it on the floor".
- **Two pages draw keycaps now**, so `.key-chip` moved from `settings.css` to `styles.css`.
- **The checklist's old `li span:first-child` rule** was centring every label in a 1 ch box the
  moment the row grew a second span. A browser showed it; no test could.
- **`LessonPogo` had no test at all**, which is how its thesis came to restate two
  simulation-measured constants in English four lines below the same numbers printed as digits.

## Ratified here, for the next session

- The floor is a page and not a stop, until someone decides the map is worth redrawing.
- Existing saves are grandfathered, by materialising the sheet rather than inferring it.
- The row is the unit of the remap. A row that needs two controls shows two.
- The floor's capture takes whichever hand she uses; the bench's two sections stay narrow.
- An extraction that rewords is not an extraction. The offer sentence on Setup keeps its lower-case
  "jump", "attack", "dash" in its own three-word table rather than borrowing the capitalised one.

## Open, and not decided here

- **Whether the floor should be reachable from the map.** Today it is not — the sign names chapters,
  and the floor is not one. A chip in Setup's answer card, or an entry in the site header beside
  Settings, would fix it. Nobody has asked for either.
- **Whether "and it's not working" meant what this sprint took it to mean.** One sentence from him
  settles it either way.
