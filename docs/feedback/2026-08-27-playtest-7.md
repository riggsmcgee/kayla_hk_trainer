# Playtest 7 — user notes (2026-08-27)

The round that designed the **ending**, and opened a second front: a way for the user to edit every
word on the site without touching code.

It follows `2026-08-26-playtest-6.md`, whose priority 5 (the boss win phase, then the ending
sequence) was half-built the same day — `66a89ac` on `proactive/2026-08-26-1646` made 1:30 a finish
line and put both Bills on a knee. **This round redesigns the half that shipped and specifies the
half that did not.**

Format as before: the user's words verbatim, then what the interview changed, then the arithmetic,
then the ratified decisions, then the work nobody asked for that the decisions require anyway.

## What the interview changed, beyond confirming instincts

**The Bills must stop, not kneel — and that is a change to code that shipped hours earlier.**
`66a89ac` puts both Bills on a knee the instant the clock reaches 1:30. That kills the fake-out
completely: a kneeling Bill tells her she has won before the walk-on has any chance to frighten her.
`theyConcede()` moves from the win event to the kneel beat, seven and a half seconds later.

**The user rejected the scariest version of his own idea.** The strongest fake-out puts up a
wave-style card naming the enemies — the game lying to her outright. It was put to him alongside a
silent version and he took the silence, which is also the version the site can stand behind: **the
dojo has never once told her something untrue**, and the last twenty seconds of it is a strange place
to start.

**"HELP!" was already in the code, and was the wrong line anyway.** `drawBarking()` draws Bill
shouting HELP! at 0:30 with WOOF! answering from the right edge, on a two-stage clock. Reusing it
verbatim at 1:30 was the obvious move — the fight has already taught her that a Bill calling for
backup **gets** backup, so the fear becomes a conclusion she draws herself rather than one the game
manufactures. The user caught what that reuse would cost: HELP! reads as Bill **losing**, and the
beat needs him escalating. Same machinery, new words.

**A likeness became available, because he changed the scale rather than the style.** Playtest 6
concluded "a likeness is not available at this resolution" and specified a silhouette plus one
oversized prop, exactly as Bill works. That conclusion was reached against **Bill's** 160 px body. At
500 px waist-up it stops being true, and the user's instinct — same style, different density — is
arithmetically right. See the table below.

**The copy tooling question had a wrong answer that looks like the right one.** An in-site editor,
where she clicks any text in the real app and edits it in place, is the obvious response to "click
the site and adjust text". It **structurally cannot reach canvas text** — those strings are pixels,
not DOM — which is precisely the half the user named: "you passed", "you failed", the cards.

## The arithmetic

### Riggs at 500 px is a different medium unless the cell changes

Bill the man: **160 px** tall on an **8 px** master cell (nothing finer than 4 px) = 20 cells; head 3
cells = **24 px**; face = (24/4)² = **36 sub-cells**, of which he spends about five on his whole
expression.

Riggs waist-up at 75% of the 664 px canvas is about **500 px**. A waist-up figure is roughly three
head-heights, so his head lands near **165 px** — larger than Bill's entire body.

| master cell   | finest detail | head is   | face is about    | vs Bill's 36                           |
| ------------- | ------------- | --------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 8 px (Bill's) | 4 px          | ~20 cells | ~1,700 sub-cells | **47x** — stops being pixel art        |
| **16 px**     | **8 px**      | ~10 cells | ~400 sub-cells   | **~11x** — an expression, still blocks |
| 24 px         | 12 px         | ~7 cells  | ~190 sub-cells   | ~5x — Bill scaled up, room wasted      |

**16 px is the choice.** It is the only one that reads as _the same medium as the Bills at a
different scale_ rather than as a different art style.

### Twenty seconds is 7.1x the longest beat the fight has

Bill's entrance is **2.8 s** and the dog's card **2.5 s**, and playtest 6 argued 2.8 s was affordable
at roughly **114 repeats per ten minutes** of grinding. 20 / 2.8 = **7.1**. But this beat fires only
after a flawless 1:30, so its repeat rate is a fraction of the entrance's — and it is still the
reason the hold-to-hurry escape exists after the first win.

### There are about 256 user-visible strings and no module holding any of them

| where                                          | count                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| page / component files carrying copy           | **19**                                                                 |
| user-visible strings of 15+ characters         | **~256**                                                               |
| `fillText` calls outside tests                 | **42** — bossSession 17, dodgeArena 13, pogoCourse 9, render 2, demo 1 |
| of those, template literals with interpolation | **9**                                                                  |
| existing copy / strings / i18n module          | **0**                                                                  |

**Nothing can show the user "all the writing" until the writing has names.** The extraction is the
load-bearing step, not the artifact.

## Ratified decisions

### The ending (note 1), in order

| at         | what                                                                                                                                                                                 | for   |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----- |
| **1:30.0** | Clock stops dead. Both Bills stop attacking. Bill's foam finger goes up (`swatTell`) and he shouts **"ALL RIGHT—"**. She cannot move. **No win text of any kind.**                   | 1.2 s |
| **+1.2**   | **"EVERYBODY!"** lands as the first body appears at a wall. The whole roster walks in from both sides. Otherwise **total silence**.                                                  | 5.0 s |
| **+6.2**   | **They arrive, and nothing happens.** All five on their marks, idling, surrounding her. This is the peak of the fear, and it exists only because the sequence was stretched to 20 s. | 1.5 s |
| **+7.7**   | **Everyone kneels, the Bills included.**                                                                                                                                             | 2.0 s |
| **+9.7**   | The kneel, held.                                                                                                                                                                     | 1.5 s |
| **+11.2**  | **She rises**, drifting to the horizontal centre of the screen as she goes.                                                                                                          | 2.6 s |
| **+13.8**  | **Everyone stands and applauds.** The spitter starts firing its volley straight up; the shots burst into confetti at the top, on a ~2 s cycle.                                       | 3.0 s |
| **+16.8**  | **Bill the dog starts backflipping**, and never stops. Bill the man applauds throughout.                                                                                             | —     |
| **+19.5**  | Prompt fades in: **"Press {jump} for what's next"**, live label. Held as long as she likes.                                                                                          | —     |

- **The Bills STOP at 1:30, they do not kneel.** This **strikes** the behaviour shipped in `66a89ac`.
  A kneeling Bill announces the win and leaves the walk-on with nothing to frighten her with.
- **Bill summons; he does not call for help.** Same two-stage `drawBarking()` clock, new words: "ALL
  RIGHT—" then "EVERYBODY!". The fight taught her at 0:30 that a Bill shouting produces
  reinforcements, so the fear is her own inference rather than something the game manufactures.
- **The game never lies.** No wave card, no enemy names, no false label. Silence is both the honest
  version and the frightening one — the fear comes from the game refusing to speak.
- **Everything keeps its idle animation for the whole twenty seconds.** At this length, with a frozen
  clock and no text, breathing bodies are the only thing separating "an ominous pause" from "the game
  has crashed". It is free — they already idle — and it stops being optional here.
- **Kneeling is a TRANSFORM, not five new painters.** A walker is a shell with leg nubs, a flier is a
  hovering ball, a spitter has no legs. One function lowers each body toward the floor and tips it
  forward toward her, around its feet, whatever the silhouette. This is also the honest answer for the
  flier: a hovering thing that sinks and tips reads as deference where a bent knee cannot. It keeps
  playtest 6's refusal to thread a pose parameter through `drawEnemy` intact.
- **The dog applauds first, then backflips.** Both poses are used: he kneels with everyone, stands and
  applauds with everyone, and only after a beat starts flipping. The flip lands as a **punchline**
  rather than as texture, and it gives the held tableau a beat change instead of one held picture.
  Bill the man applauds throughout — he is the steady one, the dog is the idiot, which is the joke.
- **She is drawn exactly as she always is.** `drawKnight` varies on two things — dashing, and facing —
  and gains no triumphant pose. She has been that 48 px silhouette for every second of the dojo, and
  giving her a new one at the end makes her someone else at the moment she should most be herself. She
  rises **and** drifts to the horizontal centre, so the tableau is symmetric around her.
- **Confetti is continuous and needs no comfort exception.** The spitter re-fires on a ~2 s cycle so
  something is always drifting through the held tableau. Colours are **steady** — no strobe, no
  full-screen flash — so `reduceFlashing` needs no special case; if it is on the palette softens, but
  she still gets her party rather than a toggle taking it away.
- **The prompt uses the same button she has pressed all along.** `jump = forward, attack = again` is
  ratified on every overlay, and the label is already live — it prints `Z`, or `Space` if she has
  rebound it, or "the bottom button" on a pad. The user's "Press 1" meant _the continue button_:
  "just keep it consistent, because by that point she'll know what you mean."
- **Unskippable the first time; hold-to-hurry at 2.5x afterwards.** Twenty seconds is 7.1x Bill's
  entrance. The first win is protected completely; every win after it can be hurried by holding
  forward, exactly as `stepIntro` / `INTRO_FAST_FORWARD` already does for the entrance. Magic once, a
  tax afterwards.
- **It replays on every win**, like the entrances.

### The credits (note 1, second half)

- **The ending hands off to a real page, not a canvas beat.** `#/the-end`. The deciding fact is that
  **there is no pixel font anywhere in this project** — every canvas string is `system-ui` — so an
  8-bit Riggs speaking in the HUD font reads as a placeholder, which playtest 6 already flagged. The
  Bills already break the palette on purpose ("Uncle Bill is from a different game"), so a handoff out
  of the arena reads as _arriving somewhere_, not as a seam.
- **Riggs is drawn, not photographed**: waist-up, ~500 px, **16 px master cell, nothing finer than
  8 px**, stylistically Bill the man's medium. His motion stays **stepped** — a two-frame blink, a
  two-frame nod. If he moves smoothly while the Bills do not, he stops looking like he is from the
  same world as them, which is the one thing his art has to do.
- **The bow tie is yellow, but NOT `punishGold`.** `#e8c76a` is taught in so many words by the Reading
  Enemies lesson — "gold — the punish window" — and is the colour of every forward button on the site.
  A tie in that gold is the picture telling her to hit him. It takes a warmer, deeper yellow that
  belongs to him and to nothing else, the same move Bill's OSU scarlet foam finger makes against an
  arena of blues.
- **Four message boxes, advanced with the same forward button.** Draft, to live in the copy deck and
  be edited there forever:
  1. "Kayla — you did it. 1:30 against both Bills, and they never laid a finger on you."
  2. "I want to be straight with you about what that means. That fight is harder than a lot of the
     real bosses in Hollow Knight. I built it that way. And you beat it clean."
  3. "Everything you just did — the bouncing, the waiting, the reading before the hitting — is the
     same thing the real game is asking you for. You've got it now."
  4. "I'm really proud of you. Go enjoy Hollow Knight. It's one of my favourite games in the world and
     I hope this made it more fun. — Riggs"
- **It is literally a credits roll.** Riggs's message, then a cast list: _Bill the Man, as himself.
  Bill the Dog, as himself._ The five enemies by name. Built for Kayla, by Riggs. She has spent the
  whole dojo learning those seven by name, and the Two Bills getting a screen credit for playing
  themselves is the joke landing one last time.
- **Settings gains "Watch the credits"** once `finaleBossCleared`. She will want to show someone, and
  a message that can only be read by first beating a boss again is the wrong shape for what it is.

### The copy pass (note 2)

- **Extraction first, then the ending.** The ending adds a pile of new writing — the shout, the
  prompt, the four boxes, the credits roll. Writing that straight into a module the user can already
  edit is free; writing it inline means extracting it again a week later.
- **One copy module, generated deck.** Every short string and every canvas string moves into a single
  named module. The deck is **generated from it**, not transcribed, so it cannot silently miss a
  string or drift after an edit.
- **Everything appears in the deck; only the short strings move into the module.** The lesson prose is
  long paragraphs woven through JSX with links and emphasis inside them — pulling those into keys
  makes them worse to edit, not better. They appear in the deck and are editable there; they keep
  living in their pages.
- **A live artifact, not a mockup and not an in-site editor.** Confirmed available this session: the
  `artifact` runtime capability (contract 0.2.27) lets a published page save new versions of itself.
  The user edits a line in place, saves, the page republishes, and the next session reads it back and
  applies it. Edits persist between sessions.
  - **Rejected: an in-site dev editor.** It is the obvious answer and it cannot touch canvas text —
    pixels, not DOM — which is exactly the half the user named.
  - **Rejected for this job: the Claude Design canvas.** It is a mockup that does not know about the
    real strings, and a 2.5 s card has no natural artboard. It is the right tool later, for
    re-_laying out_ a screen rather than re-_wording_ it.
- **Each entry shows where it appears, what makes it appear, and the text.** After an edit the
  original stays visible underneath, greyed. A pass over 250 strings across several sittings is
  exactly when you need to see what you have already touched — and it means the next session applies a
  list of **changes**, not a diff of everything.

## Hidden work — none of it is optional, none of it is in the notes

- **The fast-forward flag is the 1:30 remount bug waiting to happen again.** Hold-to-hurry after the
  first win depends on `finaleBossCleared` — and `onPassed()` **sets** `finaleBossCleared` at the
  moment she wins. A live dependency there rebuilds the session mid-celebration, which is verbatim the
  defect fixed in `819c0ea`. `PlayWell.tsx` already has the pattern: `clearedBefore`, read once with
  `useState` and frozen for the life of the beat. **Use that value, never `progress.finaleBossCleared`
  directly.**
- **`theyConcede()` fires on the `won` event today and must move.** It also plants both Bills on the
  floor, zeroes their velocity, clears projectiles and turns them to face her — all still needed, but
  at **+7.7 s**, not at 1:30. What happens at 1:30 is the stop and the shout.
- **`EndingBeat` is a two-member union and becomes six.** `ending.ts` currently holds
  `'concede' | 'cheer'`; the new sequence is stop → gather → hold → kneel → rise → cheer, and the
  cheer itself has an internal beat where the dog starts flipping.
- **`Enemy.celebrating` is `'concede' | 'applaud'` and needs more states**, or the reverence transform
  needs its own channel. It is deliberately a channel the simulation never writes — that is what keeps
  the tableau harmless without switching damage off — and any new state must keep that property.
- **The dog's backflip leaves the standing rig.** `BillDogPose` splits into rig poses
  (`idle`/`walkIn`/`bonesTell`/`bones`/`bow`/`applaud`/`lieDown`) and the two that do not use it
  (`rollTell`/`roll`). A flip belongs with the second group, and the ball's existing spin is the
  nearest precedent.
- **`drawEnemy` has no pose parameter and must not gain one.** The reverence transform wraps the call
  (translate and rotate about the feet, then draw), so no painter learns about the ending.
- **The Knight's rise must move `player.position`, not the draw call.** `render` lerps
  `prevFeet → player.position` by `alpha`; a rise applied only at draw time will jitter on any display
  that is not exactly 60 Hz. Same class of bug as the dog being invisible on his own card, fixed in
  `cda951e`.
- **`renderBills.test.ts` uses fixed literal state tables**, so a new pose adds no row and nothing goes
  red. This has now lagged reachability twice (`uncurl`, then the celebration poses). **Extend the
  tables in the same commit as the feature.**
- **The 20 s sequence is unreachable in a test** without the dev seam. `playTheEnding` exists
  (`66a89ac`) and jumps to one step short of the finish line untouched; it is the only way a test — or
  a human — can see the ending without a flawless 1:30. Nine tests already ride it.
- **Nine of the 42 `fillText` calls are template literals**, so the copy module has to hold _functions_
  for those, not strings, and the deck has to show a worked example rather than a raw placeholder.

## Open, and deliberately not decided here

- **The exact yellow of the bow tie.** Ruled out: `punishGold #e8c76a`. Not yet picked: what replaces
  it. It is a colour the user should see rather than name.
- **Where the five cast marks actually sit.** Playtest 6's correction stands and must be used: the cast
  is **394 px of drawn ink, not the 320 px `ENEMY_SIZES` reports**, and nine bodies leave **53.8 px per
  gap** across the 1168 px arena. `ENEMY_SIZES` is the collision box.
- **Whether the 5.0 s walk-on is long enough to be frightening.** The user's own instruction was "don't
  rush it", and this is the number to watch first when he plays it.
- **Whether anyone has looked at the shipped knee yet.** `agent-browser` failed on cold start twice in
  a row, so the poses from `66a89ac` are proved by tests and by nothing else. Tests cannot judge a
  pose — that is the lesson from the round before this one.
