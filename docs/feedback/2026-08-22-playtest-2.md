# Playtest 2 — user notes, diagnosis, and the ratified decisions (2026-08-22)

The developer's second annotated run through the site (eight screenshots with red notes,
received late on 2026-08-21 / early 2026-08-22), followed by a three-round `/grilling`
interview that turned the notes into decisions. This file records **what was said**, **what in
the code causes it**, and **what we decided** — the interview's conclusions were never written
down by the session that ran it, so this is the canonical record. PLAN.md §5–§8 carry the
resulting design and milestone changes.

## The eight notes

### 1. Dodge Arena should be a game, not a test bench

> "Having the option to switch between which enemy I'm facing is very nice for development
> purposes. I think in the actual final project it should be more of a game where, after she's
> able to survive past a certain point, it automatically moves on to the next level. Not saying
> it moves on past a certain time, but if she survives beyond 5 minutes, 5 minutes is probably a
> long time. If she survives beyond 60 seconds or 2 minutes, then she gets to go on to the next
> one."

**Cause:** `web/src/pages/PlayDodge.tsx` is a picker (five enemy chips + an observe checkbox)
in front of a single-enemy session (`web/src/engine/dodgeArenaSession.ts`) that only ends on
the first hit. There is no notion of passing an enemy or of a sequence.

**Decision:** the arena becomes a **staged game**. Roster in teaching order (walker → flier →
duelist → spitter → warden). Each stage is passed by **surviving 60 s AND landing clean hits**
(5 on the dummies, 3 on the attackers) — "hit them more than they hit you", literally. Passing
auto-advances to the next enemy. Getting hit restarts the *current* enemy (checkpoint per
enemy, never back to enemy 1). Reopening the page resumes at the first enemy not yet cleared.
The enemy-picker chips and the observe checkbox stay as **dev tools** (collapsed, clearly
labelled) with a standing note to **remove them in the final build**; observe mode is out of
the progression entirely.

### 2. Pogo off a dying enemy gives no bounce

> "If you pogo off an enemy and the enemy dies, then you do not get the jump from the pogo. You
> get the jump if the enemy survives the hit but if they die from the hit, you don't get the
> jump. You should get the jump."

**Cause (confirmed in code):** in `dodgeArenaSession.step`, `world.pogoables` is set from the
enemy's position *before* `stepEnemy` moves it; `stepPlayer` runs the pogo check against that
stale box, then `stepArena` runs the damage check against the *moved* box. When the nail first
connects after the move, the damage lands and the enemy dies, and the next step finds a dead
enemy that is no longer a pogo target. A survivor gets its bounce one step late; a kill never
does. Hollow Knight gives the bounce on the hit itself.

**Decision:** the bounce is triggered from the **same nail contact that deals the damage**
(one contact, both effects), so killing blows bounce too. Engine fix, test-first.

### 3. More Pogo levels — red hazard orbs, moving targets

> "Can we make more Pogo levels? I really like what we have so far but I think we can expand
> upon that. I think what we have currently is a perfect level 1. Nothing needs to be changed at
> all. … Maybe we can have a bubble with a slightly different color, maybe a little bit red, that
> you can't hit with your body. You can hit it with your needle but it hurts you so you can't
> bounce as low as you can with the blue ones. Also a level with moving platforms, not moving
> platforms, like moving things that she has to pogo off of."

**Cause:** `web/src/engine/course.ts` holds exactly one hand-placed level (`POGO_COURSE_1`);
the session is hard-wired to it. Adding levels is data plus a level sequence, not an engine
rewrite — but the course model has no hazard orbs and no moving targets.

**Decision:** **Level 1 stays exactly as is.** Three levels in the Bounce Bog:
- **Level 2 — red orbs.** A red orb is a *hazard* orb: your **body** touching it counts like
  spikes (back to the last lantern); your **nail** bounces off it exactly as strongly as off a
  blue one. The difference is purely how low you're allowed to get. Teaches bouncing early.
- **Level 3 — drifting orbs.** Blue orbs that drift along fixed paths — horizontal, vertical,
  and circles — deterministic, no RNG. Teaches timing on a moving target. (Enemies in the
  course are a later idea, not this.)
- **Level 4 — "put it all together"** — red + drifting + longer gaps — lives in the finale
  (note 4 / interview), not in the Bounce Bog.

**Clear-to-unlock**: level N must be cleared to open N+1, with the same skippable lock as
everywhere else; the picker shows cleared levels plus the next one. Replaying a cleared level
is "practice".

### 4. The jump feels right now

> "The jump height feels so much better after your changes. Good work."

No action. The continuous-release fix from playtest 1 stays as is.

### 5. Complete one topic before the next; ask which controller in chapter 1

> "The Pogo Challenge should be completed before moving on to the next section. I don't think it
> should be mandatory but I do think it should be structured in a way where it's very clear that
> you're supposed to complete one topic before moving on to the next. Even in the first section
> we can just ask her, 'Which controller do you plan on using?'"

**Cause:** a map stop lights up the first time its page is opened (`useMarkVisited`); nothing
distinguishes *visited* from *done*, and nothing on a chapter page says what "done" means.

**Decision (the biggest change — it reshapes the road):** the course is **learn → prove →
practice → next**, made visible as **one road of six stops** in that order:

1. **Dirtmouth — Your Setup** · *done when she answers "Which controller will you use?"* The
   answer is recorded and shown; it changes nothing else (it proves she's committed to one).
2. **The Crossroads — Pogo** (lesson) · ends with one big **"Prove it →"** into the Bounce Bog ·
   *done when the Pogo Course is cleared.*
3. **The Bounce Bog — Pogo Course** (levels 1–3) · *done when all three are cleared.*
4. **Greenpath — Reading Enemies** (lesson) · **"Prove it →"** into the Colosseum · *done when
   the roster is cleared.*
5. **Kbug's Colosseum — Dodge Arena** (the staged game from note 1) · *done when all five enemies
   are cleared.*
6. **The Bottom of the Well** — the finale (new; see the interview section below).

**Gates are clear-to-unlock and always skippable:** a stop whose predecessor isn't finished
shows the rule ("Finish the Pogo Course first") with the obvious button pointing back at the
challenge and a **"Skip this challenge"** in smaller text beneath it. A skipped stop counts as
passed for unlocking but is drawn as unfinished on the map. Nothing ever traps her.

The map distinguishes **done** (lit, full), **skipped/visited** (unfinished), and **locked**.
The Knight stands at the first stop that is not done.

### 6. Joy-Con: she uses the stick; say that the game is bigger than this

> "I'm pretty sure that she uses the joystick for this game not the d-pad buttons."
> "We can probably include something about how I know that there's more complexity, more
> options in this game but we're just breaking it down to these simple components."

**Cause:** `JoyConDiagram` (`web/src/components/ControllerDiagrams.tsx`) highlights the left
↓ *button* as the pogo input and the Setup copy says "pogo from the ↓ button, not the stick".

**Decision:** the diagram highlights the **stick** as hers (move, and ↓ + attack = pogo). The
↓-button advice survives as a *tip*, because it's the one Joy-Con trick that actually fixes a
pogo problem: "if pogos keep coming out as side-slashes, the ↓ button is a more reliable down."
The Setup chapter gains one short paragraph saying out loud that Hollow Knight has more in it —
focus, spells, healing — and that the dojo skips all of it on purpose to drill the few moves
that carry the whole game.

### 7. Text is a bit small; "Next stop" should be a button

> "Text size is a bit small across the board." (Clarified in the interview: "the body text feels
> fine. It's more like the fine print, small stuff around the edge of the site that's just hard
> to read. You can try 18 pixels and see how that goes.")
> "Make this an actual button." (on the chapter strip's "Next stop: …" link)

**Cause:** `web/src/styles.css` sets body at 1rem (16 px) and the fine print (chapter strip,
legend, canvas note, eyebrows, signature, map labels) at 0.7–0.92 rem — 11–15 px. The chapter
strip's next-stop is a bare `<Link>`.

**Decision:** root size to **18 px**; the fine print gets a floor so nothing around the edges
sits below ~15 px, and the things she actually reads (chapter strip, canvas note, legend,
muted paragraphs) sit at ~17 px. Headings scale with rem. If it still feels small, it's a
one-line change. The chapter strip's "Next stop" becomes a real `.button`.

### 8. The map landed

> "Amazing changes. I love the adjustments here."

No action on the map's look. The map gains a sixth stop and the done/skipped/locked states
from note 5, and its hero line updates from "three chapters, two mini-games" to the six-stop
road.

## The interview (three rounds, ratified)

Round 1 settled the notes; rounds 2–3 settled what they opened up. Everything below is the
user's decision, not a recommendation.

- **Governing principle (new):** the site teaches a *mindset* plus a few technical skills for
  the problems she has right now. It is not a model of the whole game — **no double jump**
  (she is about to get it in the real game; the trainer doesn't need it), no focus/spells/
  healing, and the Setup page says so out loud. Nothing is built "for the whole game".
- **Arena engagement (the user's own worry):** "it's just so easy to avoid everything and just
  not go near the enemies… I basically don't use the Observe Mode at all." Decision: **(a) hits
  are required to pass a stage** (5 on dummies, 3 on attackers) **and (b) every enemy hunts
  her** — closes in steadily so there is no safe corner. (Several enemies at once is reserved
  for the finale; a shrinking arena was rejected.) Observe mode leaves the progression and
  becomes a free-play toggle next to the dev picker, same remove-in-final-build note.
- **60 seconds everywhere**, dummies and attackers alike. One number to understand and to brag
  about.
- **Death = checkpoint.** Restart the current enemy / current level / current wave. Never the
  whole roster.
- **The finale — "The Bottom of the Well"** (spoiler-free: named by the map's own metaphor,
  not a real place; it pays off "Kayla, it starts at the well"). Contents, in order: **pogo
  level 4** ("put it all together"), then a **flat, platform-less arena** with **waves** —
  walker+flier → duelist+spitter → spitter+warden — each wave survive 60 s + hits, checkpointed
  per wave; then **the boss**. The boss moves from "explicitly not planned" to **planned, after
  the waves exist, designed in its own conversation with the user**. Do not build it without
  that conversation.
- **Settings page (new, reached from the Setup chapter and the header):** keyboard remapping
  (the stored-bindings shape `SettingsV1.inputBindings` already exists), the two comfort
  toggles move there, and "reset my progress". The verify-and-remap flow for real controllers
  lands with M7, when the Switch Pro and leverless are physically present. No "wings" toggle.
- **Personal bests:** one line per cleared level / enemy on the map's next-stop sign and on the
  mini-game pages — fastest clear per level, most hits / longest survival per enemy. Runs are
  already recorded; this is display only.
- **Dev tools to remove in the final build:** the arena enemy picker and the observe toggle.
- **Priority order, if a session runs out of night:** fixes (notes 2, 6, 7) → progression (road,
  done states, gates, "Prove it →", controller question, bests) → the arena as a game → pogo
  levels 2–3 → Settings → The Bottom of the Well.

## Outcome (Session 6, 2026-08-22)

Everything above is implemented except the two things deliberately deferred (the boss; the
controller verify-and-remap flow, which is M7). Details of *where* live in PLAN.md §7 (M6.6) and
the commit; the short version:

- **Built:** six-stop road with done / skipped / visited / locked states and skippable gates;
  "Prove it →" on both lessons; the controller question; staged Dodge Arena (60 s + hits,
  hunting enemies, checkpoint per enemy, dev tools collapsed); Pogo Course levels 1–3 with red
  hazard orbs and drifting orbs, clear-to-unlock; The Bottom of the Well (level 4 → three waves
  on a flat arena → a quiet placeholder for the boss); Settings (keyboard remap, comfort toggles,
  reset progress); pogo-on-kill bounce; Joy-Con stick diagram + tip; 18 px type; the Next-stop
  button. 385 tests, typecheck/lint/build green.
- **Partial:** personal bests show one line per stop / selected level / current enemy, not a
  list per cleared level and enemy (data is recorded; display only).
- **Tuned by simulation, not by hand:** hunting speeds (flier 150 px/s, duelist close-approach
  45 px/s — the spec's ~70 / ~80 failed the "no safe corner in 10 s" property or leaked a lunge
  into a lesson demo), levels 2–4 (bot-proven beatable), the waves. **Playtest 3 should start
  here.**
- **Review:** a 6-lens adversarial pass found 25 candidate defects, 23 confirmed and fixed —
  the ones she would have felt: clicking a level/wave chip left it focused so game keys were
  ignored; a locked page marked itself visited and moved the Knight past a lesson; the arena
  spawn sat under a ledge so the first jump/pogo bonked; a reflex Z press inside the death
  hit-stop restarted the stage before "Got you." showed; arena deaths could evict Pogo Course
  bests through the 500-run cap; emptying a key in Settings silently re-bound it to defaults.
