# Playtest 10 — the full walkthrough

**2026-08-29.** He played the whole trainer end to end and took notes as he went, then sat for a
three-round interview (`/grill-me`) before any code moved. Everything below was ratified in the
room. Where a question had two readings, the reading he chose is recorded with the reasoning that
made it his choice rather than mine.

This is also the first round where he had already **edited the site himself** between sessions —
seven files of rewritten copy, uncommitted, some of it load-bearing. That changes the shape of the
work: part of this round is repair, and the constraint on the repair is that his words are frozen.

## What the interview settled

### The duelist dives; it does not fly

He named the wrong attack and corrected himself in round two: the thing sweeping the upper map is the
**leap's dive**, not the ground lunge. `stepLeap` normalises an aim vector with `vy = Math.max(ty -
ey, 0.15)`, so a Knight airborne at roughly his perch height gives a `vy` of 0.15 against a `vx` in
the hundreds — he leaves the perch at about half a degree below horizontal and crosses the whole
1168 px canvas at 900 px/s with nothing vertical in it to read.

The interview offered two clamps: a floor on the dive **angle**, or a cap on its horizontal **reach**.
His own note said "halfway across the screen", which is a reach, so reach was recommended. **He chose
the angle, and his reason is the one that matters:**

> "having him travel in a straight line just sort of feels wrong. There's not really any equivalent
> to that sort of movement in Hollow Knight, while there is an equivalent to the diving down"

That is a fidelity argument, not a difficulty argument, and it outranks the tuning convenience —
the whole site exists to build habits that transfer to the real game, so an attack with no analogue
in Hollow Knight teaches a read she will never use again. **Ratified: a minimum dive angle of 35°
below horizontal.** Steeper is allowed; flatter is clamped. From his fixed 200 px perch that covers
about 286 px, comfortably more than the 260 px `gapRange` that provokes the leap, so he still closes
the gap he exists to close.

### Hits are a score, not a gate

`stepStage` currently requires `elapsed >= surviveSeconds && hits >= hitsRequired`. The second half
comes out everywhere — Colosseum and the Gauntlet's waves alike. He was asked how far it went and
answered with the thing the site is actually about:

> "this whole thing is about dodging and Pogo. She cannot get through the Pogo levels without using
> her attack, but if she manages to make it through the entire Gauntlet without getting hit or
> hitting another enemy a single time, that's fine with me."

So: **survive-only clears.** `hitsToPass` and the summed `hitsRequired` become dead data and come
out — a par that is not enforced is a soft requirement, which is exactly the thing being removed.
The HUD reads `hits 4 · best 7`. The cost, accepted: a first-ever run against an enemy shows
`best —` with no number to chase. That is one run per enemy.

### Thirty seconds is a Colosseum number, not a global one

`STAGE_SURVIVE_SECONDS = 60` is one constant shared by the Colosseum and the finale's waves, and the
waves schedule their reinforcements `at: 30`. Dropping it globally would mean the second half of
every wave's cast never walks in and "Reinforcements." never fires. He confirmed the narrow reading:

> "this is just in the reading enemies practice session. The Gauntlet timing feels good."

**The constant splits.** Colosseum 30 s. Waves stay 60 s. Bill stays at 1:30.

### Assist mode

Three lives, and the interview had to settle four things his note left open.

**Per attempt, not a global pool.** "Those lives will follow her through any challenge" reads either
as "the setting applies everywhere" or as "3 lives total for the whole site". The second has no good
ending — a trainer that locks her out is the opposite of the point, and one she can refill is not a
pool. Ratified: the setting applies everywhere; each attempt starts full.

**An attempt is the smallest retryable unit.** Each Colosseum enemy, each wave, each pogo level,
each Bills fight. The waves beat auto-advances, so wave 2 gets a fresh three — that unit is the one
that already restarts on death everywhere else, so it is the one she has already been taught.

**In the Pogo Course a life absorbs a hazard touch** — no walk back to the lantern. The miss is still
counted and still shown, exactly as god mode already behaves. There is no death in the course to
spend a life on otherwise.

**The discouragement is a pop-up, in his words:**

> "when she turns it on for the first time, she'll get a little pop-up asking, 'Are you sure?' and
> then something about how the site is intended to challenge her. I want her to get to experience it
> all, but also making it easier won't help her journey in Hollow Knight because there's no
> functionality in that game to make it easier."

Once ever, the first time she moves it above Off. It does not re-fire after Reset Progress — the
point is made once, and re-asking turns a considered warning into something she clicks through.

**Control:** `Assist mode — Off · 1 · 2 · 3`, under a new **Difficulty** heading in Settings.
"Assist mode" is the genre's own term for exactly this, and it is honest without "Easy" stinging.

### Assist is never punished; it is always beatable

The interview surfaced a conflict he had not been asked about: the existing precedent (`bests.ts`)
drops `godMode` runs from personal bests entirely. Applying that to assist would freeze the very
high score he had just asked for the whole time assist is on — a strong discouragement, not the
slight one he specified, and it would neuter the new feature.

**Ratified: rank them, don't exclude them.** `beats()` already breaks ties on `cleared` before it
looks at any number; `assisted` becomes a tiebreak above that. A clean run outranks an assisted one
at any hit count, an assisted best displays as `Best: 7 hits (assist)`, and the asterisk disappears
by itself the moment she does it clean.

The same shape settles the ending letter. `theEndCopy.messages` opens with "they never laid a finger
on you" and message 2 claims "you beat it clean" — true today, because one touch ends the fight, and
a lie the moment she has three. He rejected writing alternate prose:

> "keep the original line, and then if she turns on the assistant mode where she can take multiple
> hits, then just don't include that. No further change is needed."

So the untrue clauses are **omitted**, not rewritten. And because the letter is re-readable from
Settings forever, the version is decided at **render** time from her run history: if she has ever
cleared the Bills unassisted, she sees the full letter, every word as he wrote it. The clean run
permanently upgrades it. No new stored state — it is a pure function of the runs already recorded.

### Bill is a surprise, and the leak was on the map

Two live strings named him before she could ever meet him:

- `chapters.ts` — `FINALE_DONE`, which prints on the map's next-stop sign and on every gate panel.
  Becomes `Clear the level, all two waves, and whatever's waiting at the bottom`.
- `bestLine.ts` — `Best: 1:12 against the Bills`. **This one turned out to be safe and stays as
  written.** `bossBest(runs)` is null until she has a Bills run, and `finaleLine` falls back to waves
  then level, so the string cannot render before she has fought him. He set the test himself:

  > "as long as the line that says 'the best against the Bills' comes after her first attempt, then
  > that's fine. It just can't show up before she actually sees the Bills."

`theEnd.ts` names the Two Bills in Settings, but only appears once she has beaten them — not a leak.
The Gauntlet's own beat strip already reads "The thing at the bottom", which is ominous without
naming him, and stays. Code comments keep saying Bill; she never reads those.

### The dog's entrance, re-paced

Today the whole beat is 2.5 s and everything runs **at once** on one timer: the card, the shout, the
woof and the dog's walk-in all overlap. His script makes them sequential.

1. Bill shouts — everything freezes, nobody moves
2. `WOOF!` answers from off-screen
3. the dog walks in and reaches his mark — still no card
4. **then** the card appears, and holds with no timer on it
5. a press — any button, any direction — resumes the fight

About 6–7 s instead of 2.5 s, clock paused throughout as it already is.

**The one conflict, and its resolution.** "Press any button to continue" collides with the ratified
rule that holding jump *fast-forwards* an entrance at 2.5x — on a retry she is often already holding
jump, which would dismiss the card on the frame it appeared. Steps 1–3 stay fast-forwardable; step
4 requires a **fresh** press, an input that went released to pressed after the card was up. A held
button does nothing.

**Bill the man's own card** goes 0.7 s to about 2 s but gets no input gate. He flagged both cards as
too fast; two press-to-continue prompts in one entrance turns a boss fight into a slideshow, and the
dog is the surprise worth stopping for.

### The overlays name actions, not inputs

His first note is not about the binding — `jump = forward, attack = again` is already ratified and
does not change. It is about the **label**. `jumpLabel`/`attackLabel` resolve to what she has bound,
which prints "the bottom button" on a pad, and on a leverless board that is meaningless: the buttons
are not laid out as a diamond and the position vocabulary has nothing to point at.

So the in-canvas overlays stop naming the input and name the action: **`Press Jump to continue ·
Attack to retry`**, identically on keyboard and pad. The controls caption under the canvas keeps
naming real keys and buttons — that one is a reference card and the specifics are the whole point of
it.

### The dash gap, and the first hard block on the road

Level 2 gets a gap that **cannot** be crossed without pogo then dash; level 3 a wider one over a
moving target; the Gauntlet's level 4 a moving target that is **red**, which does not exist yet —
`hazardOrbs` are static and `movers` are harmless, so `Mover` gains a `hazard` flag rather than a
fourth list appearing.

"So that we know that she knows how to do that" means genuinely required, so the gap is sized by
simulation — wider than a pogo-only arc reaches, inside a pogo-plus-dash arc — and pinned by a test,
so a physics change cannot quietly make it clearable the wrong way.

**This is a change to the road's contract and it was flagged as one.** These courses are a linear
walkway of pits with no alternate route. A gap she cannot cross is the first thing on the site that
can hard-block her, and lives do not help — a life absorbs a spike, not a gap she cannot reach
across. Her only escape is skipping the whole level, which is a much larger concession than skipping
one pit. Accepted, with a **checkpoint immediately before the gap** so failing it costs a two-second
walk rather than the run, and the existing level-skip left as the ultimate escape hatch.

## Ratified here, for the next session

- A duelist attack with no analogue in Hollow Knight is a bug even when it is dodgeable. Fidelity
  outranks tuning convenience.
- Hits are a score. Survival is the gate. An unenforced par is a soft requirement and comes out.
- `STAGE_SURVIVE_SECONDS` was one constant doing two jobs. Colosseum 30 s, waves 60 s.
- Assist mode is ranked below clean play, never excluded from it. The asterisk clears itself.
- His prose is frozen; his spelling is not.
- Bill's name may not appear on any surface she can reach before the bottom of the well.

## Open, and not decided here

- **Whether the Colosseum at 30 s with no hit requirement is now too easy.** Survive half a minute
  and it clears. That is what "get to know the character" asked for, and it may want a second look
  once he has played it.
- **Whether the level-2 dash gap is the right place for the first hard block.** It is checkpointed
  and the level is skippable, but it is still the first wall on the road that cannot be walked
  through.
